# Content Product Skill

The Tier 2 deliverable context for content products.

## What this skill provides

Content products are products where the canonical artifact IS the content. Different shape than software products — instead of a spec stack + code, the load-bearing deliverables are editorial + production + distribution.

Content products can live under any pillar:
- **Product pillar** — when content IS the canonical company product (a publication, a course platform, a podcast network)
- **Brand pillar** — when content is brand expression (a brand-led podcast, a thought-leadership newsletter)

The Tier 2 deliverables are the same shape regardless of pillar.

### Tier 2 deliverables for content products

- **Editorial calendar** — what gets produced, when, on what cadence
- **Content production workflow** — from idea to publication: research → outline → draft → review → edit → publish
- **Distribution strategy** — channels, cadence, audience-by-channel
- **Audience growth plan** — how the audience expands over time
- **Editorial guidelines** — content-specific (separate from brand voice; about what content earns publication vs not)
- **Episode / issue / chapter template** — the recurring content unit's standard structure
- **Content models** — for content with structure (interview format, listicle format, etc.)
- **Source / fact-checking standards** — especially for journalism / non-fiction
- **Rights + licensing** — for content that includes others' work
- **Archive strategy** — how past content stays discoverable

### Type-specific specializations

#### Newsletter

- **Email service provider setup** (Substack, ConvertKit, Beehiiv, Ghost, etc.)
- **Subscriber acquisition funnels**
- **Open-rate + click-rate targets**
- **Free vs. paid tier structure** (if monetized)

#### Podcast

- **Recording + production pipeline** (host gear, guest setup, editing, mixing)
- **Show notes template**
- **Distribution to podcast directories** (Apple, Spotify, etc.)
- **Audiogram / clip strategy** for social
- **Guest outreach + booking workflow**

#### Book

- **Outline / structure** (chapter breakdown)
- **Writing schedule** + draft milestones
- **Editor + beta-reader process**
- **Publishing path** (traditional / self / hybrid)
- **Launch strategy** (advance reader copies, blurbs, launch promotion)
- **Audiobook + ebook strategy**

#### Course

- **Curriculum map** — modules, lessons, learning objectives
- **Production workflow** for video + supporting materials
- **Learning management system** (LMS) selection / setup
- **Student-engagement strategy** (cohorts, community, office hours)
- **Pricing model** (one-time / subscription / cohort-based)

#### Documentary

- **Story arc + treatment**
- **Subject access + release management**
- **Production schedule** (pre-prod, prod, post)
- **Distribution strategy** (festival circuit, streaming, theatrical)

#### Magazine / Publication

- **Section structure** + recurring departments
- **Contributor roster + commissioning workflow**
- **Print vs. digital strategy**
- **Issue cadence + production milestones**

#### YouTube channel

- **Video format + length conventions**
- **Thumbnail + title strategy** (algorithm-aware)
- **Posting cadence**
- **Cross-platform strategy** (TikTok / Shorts / IG Reels integration)

## What this skill emphasizes

When loaded, the AI should keep in mind:

1. **Editorial calendar is the load-bearing deliverable.** Software's load-bearing deliverable is the spec stack; content's is the calendar. Without it, content production is reactive rather than strategic.

2. **Cadence + consistency > occasional brilliance.** Content products live or die on consistency. A weekly newsletter that arrives on time, every week, beats a brilliant essay every other month.

3. **Distribution is co-equal with creation.** Content created without a distribution plan is a tree falling in the forest. The distribution strategy isn't an afterthought.

4. **Source standards matter for non-fiction.** Fact-checking + sourcing + ethics are deliverables, not just sensibilities. Audiences trust content that takes sourcing seriously.

5. **Archive matters for long-arc content.** Past content has discoverability + trust value if archived well. Most content products under-invest here.

## What's similar to software products

- Audience definition (same shape, different content)
- Brand voice (imported from Brand pillar)
- Metrics framework (engagement, retention, growth)
- Roadmap discipline (per-version foreshadowing)

## What's different from software products

- No spec stack (DATA_MODEL_SPEC, IMPL_SPEC, etc.) — replaced by editorial workflow
- No code → no deploy workflow → no articulation-closure-against-software-deliverables
- Production schedule replaces sprint cadence
- "Ship" is each content unit, not each version
