# Service Product Skill

The Tier 2 deliverable context for service products.

## What this skill provides

Service products are products where the canonical artifact is human delivery — consulting, agency work, coaching, training delivery, retainer services. Different load-bearing shape from software (spec stack + code) and content (editorial calendar):

Service products can live under any pillar:
- **Product pillar** — when service IS the canonical company offering (a consulting firm, an agency)
- **Business pillar** — when service is a commercial offering layered on top of a Product-pillar product (enterprise consulting that accompanies a software product)

### Tier 2 deliverables for service products

- **Service catalog** — the menu of offerings; what each is, who it's for, what's included, what's excluded
- **Engagement model** — proposal → SOW → onboarding → delivery → handoff → ongoing relationship
- **Delivery playbooks** — per service offering, the standard delivery process
- **Service tiers** — when offerings come in tiers (Bronze / Silver / Gold-style)
- **Pricing structure** — by engagement type (fixed-fee, hourly, retainer, value-based)
- **Case study template** — for documenting past engagements
- **Proposal template** — standard proposal format
- **SOW template** — statement-of-work format
- **Onboarding workflow** — how a new client gets oriented and started
- **Handoff template** — for engagements that conclude (vs. ongoing retainers)
- **Quality assurance criteria** — what "good delivery" means for each offering
- **Client communication cadence** — standard touch points + escalation paths

### Type-specific specializations

#### Consulting

- **Discovery process** — how engagements start
- **Recommendations framework** — how deliverables get structured
- **Stakeholder management approach**
- **Confidentiality + NDA standards**

#### Agency (creative / marketing / dev)

- **Pitch / pitch-deck template**
- **Project intake process**
- **Creative brief template**
- **Revision policy** (how many rounds)
- **Asset delivery standards**

#### Coaching

- **Intake assessment**
- **Session structure** (standard duration, format)
- **Progress tracking framework**
- **Re-engagement / continuation policy**

#### Training delivery

- **Curriculum** (overlap with content product if the training is also packaged)
- **Cohort or 1:1 delivery model**
- **Certification / completion criteria**
- **Post-training support structure**

#### Retainer services

- **Retainer SLA** (response time, capacity, scope of work)
- **Monthly check-in cadence**
- **Scope-creep management protocols**
- **Renewal / re-engagement workflow**

#### Professional services (enterprise SaaS context)

- **Implementation playbook** — how customers get the product working
- **Integration patterns** — common technical integration shapes
- **Customer success handoff** — when PS ends and CS begins
- **Pricing tied to product tiers**

## What this skill emphasizes

When loaded, the AI should keep in mind:

1. **The service catalog is the load-bearing deliverable.** It's what clients buy. Vague catalogs produce vague engagements which produce scope disputes.

2. **Pricing structure shapes the whole business.** Fixed-fee vs. hourly vs. retainer vs. value-based each produce very different operational shapes. Choose deliberately.

3. **Delivery playbooks beat heroic delivery.** Without playbooks, service quality depends on the specific deliverer. With playbooks, the org can scale beyond founder-delivery.

4. **Handoff is a deliverable.** Engagements end. How they end determines whether the client comes back, refers others, becomes a testimonial. Underweight this and the business stays acquisition-dependent.

5. **Service products generate Product-pillar opportunities.** Pattern-recognition across many engagements often surfaces productizable solutions. Capture these — they're the bridge from service business to product business.

## What's similar to other product types

- Audience definition (clients = audience)
- Brand voice (imported from Brand pillar — applies to proposals, deliverables, client comms)
- Metrics framework (utilization, billable %, NPS, repeat-engagement rate)

## What's different

- No spec stack (or much lighter — per-engagement scope is the spec)
- No code or content cadence — delivery is per-engagement
- Capacity is the binding constraint (delivery is human-time-bound)
- Quality depends on practitioners more than on artifacts
