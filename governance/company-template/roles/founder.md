# Founder

> **Purpose:** The pre-CEO / pre-pillar-lead state. A founder is whoever started the entity and is currently doing whatever the company needs. Founders play multiple roles simultaneously; the role taxonomy formalizes as the company grows.

---

## Primary pillar + secondary pillars

- **Primary pillar:** Company-wide.
- **Secondary pillars:** Whatever the company most needs at the moment — often all three.

A founder's pillar coverage shifts based on company stage. Early on, a founder is the entire company; in growth-stage, the founder evolves into a specific role (most often CEO; sometimes CTO if technical, CPO if product-led, etc.).

---

## Typical responsibilities

### The work
A founder's responsibilities are *whatever the company most needs that isn't being done*. Concretely, this usually means:

- Building the first version of the product (especially technical founders)
- Selling to the first customers (especially commercial founders)
- Hiring the first employees (every founder)
- Raising the first capital (every founder)
- Defining the initial brand identity (especially design-led founders)
- Setting up the legal entity + early ops infrastructure
- Owning the strategic narrative
- Being the public face of the company
- Doing whatever else is on fire that day

### What a founder DOESN'T have
- A defined job description
- A clear reporting structure (founders typically report to co-founders + the board + investors collectively)
- A clean separation of pillars (founders cross all three constantly)
- Defined working hours
- A defined compensation structure (often deferred / equity-heavy)

---

## Typical deliverables

Founder-authored deliverables in the early stages:

- **Founding narrative** — the why-this-team / why-now / why-this story
- **First version of the product** — typically the MVP / prototype
- **First idea-dumps + research synthesis** — the initial articulation
- **First vision spec + version spec** — pre-formal-methodology, often light
- **Founding term sheet + cap table**
- **First customer conversations / sales conversations**
- **First hires + their offer letters**
- **Initial brand identity** — name, logo hypothesis, voice hypothesis
- **Initial pitch deck** — for capital + recruiting
- **Initial business hypothesis** — market thesis, model hypothesis

As the company formalizes, deliverables migrate to pillar leads. The founder retains ownership of:
- Strategic direction (with co-founders)
- The hire of pillar leads
- External narrative
- Cultural tone

---

## Reporting + collaboration patterns

### Reports to
- The board (when formed)
- Lead investors (pre-board)
- Co-founders (peer)

### Reports to the founder
- Initially: everyone in the company
- Eventually: the executive team (pillar leads)

### Collaborates with
- **Co-founders** — peer relationship; often the most important professional relationship in the company's early years
- **Earliest investors / advisors** — capital + counsel
- **First employees** — close working relationship; often defines the cultural template
- **Earliest customers** — direct conversations that shape product direction

---

## Stage variations

### Pre-incorporation
- Founder is alone (or with co-founders) building a hypothesis
- No formal structure; the work is "validate the idea"

### Pre-product
- Entity incorporated; founder is doing everything
- Work: prototype, raise pre-seed, recruit co-founders if not present
- No employees; sometimes contractors

### Post-product, pre-PMF
- First employees hired; founder shifts from "doing everything" to "doing everything + managing the doing"
- Often the most stressful stage — founder bandwidth is the hard constraint
- Major work: find PMF, raise seed, hire first specialists

### Post-PMF, scaling
- Founder hires pillar leads (CTO, CMO, COO, etc.)
- Founder's title formalizes — often CEO; sometimes CTO/CPO/CMO if a co-founder takes CEO
- The founder role *transitions* to whatever C-level role best fits
- "Founder" remains as a permanent designation but the operational role is the C-level title

### Mature
- "Founder" is a permanent designation
- Operational role is whatever the founder formally holds (CEO, exec chair, board chair, retired-from-day-to-day)
- Some founders remain operational; some transition to board roles; some leave entirely

## Founder transitions

The most important methodology question about founders is how the role transitions as the company matures. Common patterns:

- **Founder-to-CEO.** The default path. Founder remains in operational executive role.
- **Founder-to-CTO** (or CPO/CMO). When a co-founder takes the CEO role and the founder retains a specialist executive role.
- **Founder-to-Board Chair.** When the founder steps back from operations but remains on the board.
- **Founder departure.** Often happens at acquisition, IPO, or strategic pivot the founder doesn't lead.

The methodology rule: **founders evolve into C-level roles as the role taxonomy matures.** A "Founder" with no formal C-level role at a 200-person company is a methodology smell — usually a sign the structural conversation hasn't happened.

## What the founder is NOT (especially in growth-stage)

Once pillar leads are hired, the founder should explicitly NOT be:

- **The pillar leads' direct manager-of-everything.** Founders often retain manager-of-managers patterns from the early stage; this becomes a bottleneck.
- **The decider-of-everything.** Pillar leads need delegated authority; founders need to step back from per-decision involvement.
- **The single source of cultural tone.** Cultural tone scales through pillar leads + practices, not through founder presence.

These transitions are difficult and often where companies stumble. The methodology should support founder evolution explicitly.
