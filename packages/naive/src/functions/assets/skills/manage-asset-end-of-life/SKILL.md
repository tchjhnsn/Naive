# manage-asset-end-of-life

You find the items that are at the end of their life, cost the options, find the lawful route and keep the evidence. The founder decides what leaves, at what price, and signs anything a government body or a carrier needs signed. You never wipe, sell, ship or dispose of anything on your own say.

Two rules run through the whole group. Data is destroyed before an item leaves, and that destruction is a founder decision because it cannot be undone. And nothing is disposed of without evidence of where it went, because the venture stays answerable for an item after it has left the door.

## The processes

### 10.4.1 Develop exit strategy

Runs `workflows/10-4-1-develop-exit-strategy.json`.

Read the register for items past or within six months of their useful life, warranty end or lease end, and bring the repair history with each. Then put keep, repair, replace and do without side by side over the next two years with the cost of each, the time lost when the item fails, and the resale value it still has. The trap is costing a replacement without the trade-in or resale, which usually makes replacing look worse than it is. The choice is the founder's as a `spend-money` decision.

### 10.4.2 Decommission productive assets

Runs `workflows/10-4-2-decommission-productive-assets.json`.

Before an item stops being used, list what still depends on it, what replaces it, what must be returned under a lease or loan, and what data it holds. Set it to in-storage once it is out of use, or to disposed with its disposal filled in when it has already gone. The quiet cost here is everything that kept billing after the item stopped working: a licence, a plan, insurance, a connection. List each one for the founder as a `cancel-or-downgrade` decision.

### 10.4.3 Perform sale or trade

Runs `workflows/10-4-3-perform-sale-or-trade.json`.

Value the item from public listings and trade-in programmes with the date you read them, and say what condition and accessories those prices assume. Then prepare the sale and stop: the listing, the buyer and the agreed price are the founder's, because a sale moves the venture's property and involves someone outside it. Nothing holding data is sold before it is wiped, and the record is closed afterwards with the method, the proceeds and where the receipt and wipe evidence are kept.

### 10.4.4 Manage take-back centers

Runs `workflows/10-4-4-manage-take-back-centers.json`.

For each class of item, record the maker's, retailer's or carrier's take-back, trade-in or recycling programme with what it accepts, what it pays or charges and what it requires, each with its source and date. A route is often both the cheapest option and the only one that returns proof of responsible disposal, which is why it is worth finding before the item is already in a cupboard. If the venture sells a physical product it may be asked to take back, the promise about that is a public commitment, so you draft it and the founder makes it.

### 10.4.5 Dismantle assets

Runs `workflows/10-4-5-dismantle-assets.json`.

Say why the item is being broken down rather than sold or recycled whole, then take the dangerous and data-holding pieces out first: battery, drive, refrigerant, fuel, capacitors. Record which parts are kept, which are recycled and which are waste, and where each kept part is stored. Close the parent record with its disposal and name the parts kept from it, so the register shows parts instead of a machine that no longer exists.

### 10.4.6 Track parts

Runs `workflows/10-4-6-track-parts.json`.

Keep one list of spares and salvaged parts with what each fits, its condition, how many there are, where it is and what it cost or which item it came from, and update it every time a part is used or bought. A list nobody updates is worse than no list, because a repair plans around a part that is not there. Which spares are worth holding is a judgment between what downtime costs and what holding ties up, so you recommend and the founder sets the level.

### 10.4.7 Recycle parts

Runs `workflows/10-4-7-recycle-parts.json`.

Name the route before the item moves: a maker's programme, a municipal electronics drop-off, a certified recycler, or the landlord's waste contractor. For electronics, a recycler certified under R2 or e-Stewards is the kind of route that returns usable evidence. Confirm that anything holding data was wiped or physically destroyed first, and record the route, the date and where the certificate or weight receipt is kept. A recycler's assurance is not the venture's evidence.

### 10.4.8 Ship hazardous material

Runs `workflows/10-4-8-ship-hazardous-material.json`.

Lithium batteries, devices containing them, aerosols, solvents, paints, refrigerants and compressed gas are treated as dangerous goods, and the rules sit with the carrier and the regulator, not with the venture's convenience. Collect the safety data sheet and the carrier's current published rules for each item with the date you read them, then prepare the packaging, labels, quantity limits and declaration as a draft. A person signs the declaration, and where the shipment looks like a job for a qualified shipper or a licensed disposal contractor, say so plainly and stop.

### 10.4.9 Provide government reporting

Runs `workflows/10-4-9-provide-government-reporting.json`.

List what a government body actually requires of this venture where it operates: a return on business equipment where the state or locality taxes business personal property, electronics or waste reporting where it applies, and the return any permit the venture holds asks for. Draft each return from the register, the leases and the disposal records, cite the record behind every figure, and list separately what the form asks for that no record holds. Only a person files with a government body, and the filing date and the copy are recorded afterwards. This is not legal or tax advice, and a question about what applies goes to the founder and their accountant or counsel.

### 10.4.10 Perform abandonment

Runs `workflows/10-4-10-perform-abandonment.json`.

Work from the lease's own restoration clause, not from the landlord's wish list, and list exactly what must be removed, made safe and put back. Evidence is dated photographs, the contractor's sign-off and final meter readings, because the deposit argument is decided on evidence. Assemble the handover (keys, badges and codes, drawings, manuals, certificates, meter readings, an inventory of what stays) and get written acceptance before the deposit is claimed. Any obligation that outlives the lease, such as monitoring a regulator requires, goes to the founder as an obligation and not a footnote.

### 10.4.11 Perform waste and hazardous goods management

Runs `workflows/10-4-11-perform-waste-and-hazardous-goods-management.json`.

Keep the current safety data sheet for every hazardous material the venture holds, and write in plain words what each applicable rule requires with its source and date, because these rules are local and they change. Then check that what the sheet and the rule require (storage, labelling, protective equipment, a spill kit) is actually on the shelf rather than on the list. Material that is expired, contaminated, unused or held longer than a rule allows is flagged to leave with the date the clock started; the lawful routes are costed side by side and the founder chooses, and the handler is confirmed licensed for that material before anything moves. Keep the manifests, receipts and weights, because they are asked for years later.

## Records

Read: `venture`, `assets/register`, `assets/leases`, `finance/budget`, `finance/spend`, `people`, `product`, `legal/risks`, `it/connections`, `supply-chain/suppliers`, `supply-chain/purchase-orders`, `decisions`.

Write: `assets/register/[id]` (status, and the disposal block with its date, method, whether it was wiped, the evidence and the proceeds), `assets/leases/[id]` when a lease ends or a handover changes it, `decisions/[id]`, and `operations/handoff/[id]` for the lists, routes and paperwork.

Not built: Naive has no record for a parts list, a take-back route, a hazardous material inventory, a safety data sheet, a disposal manifest, a government filing or a site monitoring obligation. Each of those goes on the handoff as evidence and into the decision it serves; the only disposal fields that persist are the ones the asset record's disposal block holds.

## Standards

- NIST SP 800-88 for sanitizing media before a device leaves, and for when physical destruction is the only sufficient method.
- ISO 55000 for managing an asset through to its disposal rather than only while it works.
- R2 and e-Stewards, the certifications that mark an electronics recycler whose evidence is worth keeping.
- OSHA's Hazard Communication Standard and the Globally Harmonized System for safety data sheets and labelling in the United States.
- The US Department of Transportation's hazardous materials regulations and the IATA Dangerous Goods Regulations for shipping, and the Resource Conservation and Recovery Act for hazardous waste, including the smaller generator categories most ventures fall into. None of this is legal advice.
- APQC Process Classification Framework 8.0, process group 10.4, for the order of this work.

## What the person decides

- Whether to keep, repair, replace or do without an item at the end of its life.
- Every wipe or physical destruction of a device that holds data, before it happens.
- Whether an item is sold, traded, dismantled or recycled, and at what price.
- Any promise to take the venture's own product back from a customer.
- Which disposal route to use where the cheapest and the lawful route differ.
- Signing a dangerous goods declaration, and filing anything with a government body.
- Cancelling the licences, plans and insurance that ran with a decommissioned asset.
- What is restored on a site the venture leaves, and accepting the handover terms.
