# Business Discovery Framework

A conversation framework for understanding another operator's business comprehensively enough to build their vault and systems on top of it. Industry-agnostic structure with industry-specific addenda.

This is the tool the consultant opens during a discovery / intake session. It is *not a script*. It's a structure that ensures the conversation surfaces what needs to be surfaced.

---

## When to use this

- First in-depth session with a new operator
- Re-engagement with an existing operator after a major business change
- Diagnostic when an existing operator feels stuck and isn't sure why

## How to use it

- **Open this doc and the checklist** (`vault-and-tooling-consultancy-setup.md`) side-by-side at the start of the session
- **Run the layers in order** for a first session; you can jump between layers in later sessions
- **Take notes directly in the operator's vault** — they see capture happening; this enacts the data sovereignty principle
- **Use their own words** wherever possible; don't translate to methodology vocabulary in real-time
- **Don't fill silences too quickly** — operators often surface the most useful answers when given thinking room

Expect 60-120 minutes for a first session covering all 7 layers. If you have less time, prioritize Layers 1, 3, 4, and 7.

---

## The seven layers

### Layer 1 — The Vivid Present

**Purpose**: get the operator talking comfortably; surface ops, customers, tools, time-use, energy, frustrations, and joys in one pass.

**Magic prompt**: *"Walk me through what a typical Tuesday looks like — from when you wake up until you stop working."*

**Follow-on prompts**:
- "Walk me through what happens when a new client first contacts you — from their first message to their first visit."
- "What's the most recent thing you did for the business, in the last 24 hours?"
- "What did this week look like? Last week?"

**What to listen for**: tools mentioned in passing, time of day they work, energy peaks/valleys, who else is involved, recurring frustrations they don't even name as frustrations.

### Layer 2 — The Full Surface

**Purpose**: factual picture of the business — what, who, when, where, how much, with whom.

**Prompts**:

- **What** — "What services do you offer, and what does each one cost?"
- **Who** — "How many active clients do you have right now? How many are recurring?"
- **When** — "What does your schedule look like? Are there busy days/seasons?"
- **Where** — "What geography do you serve? How far do you travel? Do you have a physical location?"
- **How much** — "Roughly, what's a good month vs. a tough month?" (only if comfortable — calibrate to relationship)
- **With whom** — "Are you the only person doing this, or do you have help? Contractors? Partners?"

**Optional deeper cuts**:
- "What's your client mix — long-term recurring, one-time, mix?"
- "How do new clients usually find you?"
- "What's your booking lead time — same-day, week ahead, month ahead?"

**What to listen for**: revenue model (recurring vs. transactional), capacity constraints (solo vs. team), geographic scope, customer acquisition channels.

### Layer 3 — The Pain & Friction

**Purpose**: surface where the vault + methodology will deliver immediate value. Pain points point to where to build first.

**Prompts**:

- "What part of the business takes way more time than it should?"
- "What's something that keeps slipping through the cracks?"
- "If you could wave a wand and eliminate one part of your job, what would it be?"
- "Tell me about the last time something went wrong — a missed visit, a billing issue, a lost client. What happened?"
- "What's the Sunday-night feeling about the week ahead?" *(emotional temperature check)*
- "What part of running the business do you dread?"

**What to listen for**: repeated manual tasks (automation candidates), forgotten/dropped things (capture-system candidates), high-stakes errors (process candidates), emotional weight (coaching candidates).

### Layer 4 — The Dreams (apply BASED)

**Purpose**: surface the operator's actual ambition, uncensored. This is the Phase 0C work — refuse premature narrowing.

**Prompts** (deploy progressively):

- "If you could design the business of your dreams, with zero constraints on money, time, or possibility — what does it look like in five years?"
- "What would make you say 'I made it'?"
- "What's the most ambitious version of this you've ever imagined, even if you've never said it out loud?"
- "If a customer wrote you a review you'd love to receive — what does it say?"
- "What would you build if you knew you couldn't fail?"

**Watch for self-censorship cues**:

| Cue | What it usually means | Gentle redirect |
|---|---|---|
| "I just want to..." | Pre-narrowed | "What if 'just' wasn't a limit — what would you actually want?" |
| "Realistically..." | Pre-narrowing | "Set realism aside for a sec — what's the dream?" |
| "I don't know if I could..." | Premature humility | "If you could, what would it be?" |
| "That's probably too..." | Premature dismissal | "Say it anyway — let's see what it sounds like." |
| "But the market is..." | Pre-defensiveness | "Forget the market for a moment — what's the version of this you'd build for yourself?" |

**Apply each BASED letter as a check on what you hear**:

- **Broad** — is the aperture as wide as it could be?
- **Audacious** — would they be willing to say this in public?
- **Silly** — is there a playful / Willy-Wonka-tier vivid version of this?
- **Egotistical** — are they willing to put themselves at the center?
- **Delusional** — are they refusing current evidence long enough to imagine what isn't yet?

**Note**: don't say "BASED" or explain the framework to the operator. You're applying it as the consultant; the operator just sees you asking thoughtful, generative questions.

### Layer 5 — The Gap

**Purpose**: surface what's between today (Layer 1-2) and the dream (Layer 4).

**Prompts**:

- "What's standing between you and that dream version of the business?"
- "What's one thing that, if solved, would make the biggest difference?"
- "Is the bottleneck time, money, knowledge, energy, people, or something else?"
- "What have you tried that didn't work?"
- "What do you wish you knew that you don't?"

**What to listen for**: the *specific* shape of the bottleneck. "I need more clients" is vague; "I lose every Friday to billing reconciliation" is specific. Specificity is the substrate of action.

### Layer 6 — The Next Step

**Purpose**: identify the smallest immediate action that moves the needle, so the operator walks away with momentum, not just structure.

**Prompts**:

- "If we did ONE thing today that would make this week better, what would it be?"
- "What's a small win that would feel disproportionately good?"
- "What's something you've been meaning to do but haven't gotten around to?"
- "What's the smallest version of the dream we could start on today?"

This conversation produces **the first planned session** for the operator's vault — concrete, scoped, actionable.

### Layer 7 — Tooling Inventory

**Purpose**: groundwork for connector setup; understand what already exists vs. what needs to be built.

**Prompts**:

- "Walk me through every tool, app, or piece of software you use for the business."
- "Where do client records live? Schedules? Payments? Photos? Communications?"
- "Phone setup — separate business line, or personal?"
- "How do you take payment?"
- "Business email? Social accounts? Website?"
- "What tools do you wish you used but haven't set up?"

**Categories to walk through systematically**:
- Scheduling / calendar
- Client intake / forms
- Client database / CRM
- Payment processing
- Accounting / bookkeeping
- Tax preparation
- Communications (email, SMS, social DMs)
- Photo / file storage
- Marketing (social, email lists, ads)
- Supplier portals / inventory
- Insurance / licensing portals
- Banking
- Domain / website / hosting

For each tool, capture: what's used, who else has access, what data lives there, whether they're happy with it. This feeds Phase 5 of the consultancy checklist (external tool connections).

## Capture protocol

During the session:

1. **Open her vault. Create a new file**: `_sessions/raw/<date>-discovery-session.md`
2. **Take notes there in real time**, in her own words
3. **Don't pre-categorize** — capture raw; organize later
4. **Use timestamps** for major shifts in topic (helpful when reviewing)
5. **Don't try to be comprehensive in-line** — flag for follow-up rather than chasing every thread

After the conversation, take a 10-15 minute break and synthesize:

- Pull observations into the entity-profile draft
- Pull ideas into `_ideas/`
- Pull next-steps into `_sessions/planned/`
- Pull dreams into a draft VISION_SPEC

---

## Output artifacts by end of session

By the time the operator leaves the first session, her vault should contain:

- [ ] **`entity-profile.md`** for the business (concept or scoped stage)
- [ ] **3-5 idea notes** in `_ideas/` (captured from Layers 3, 4, 5)
- [ ] **1-2 planned sessions** in `_sessions/planned/` (from Layer 6)
- [ ] **Session-log entry** for today's discovery session
- [ ] **VISION_SPEC draft** (rough — Phase 0C output from Layer 4)
- [ ] **Tooling inventory note** at `_meta/tooling-inventory.md`
- [ ] **Pain points / friction note** for prioritization
- [ ] **`code-pointer.md` placeholder** if any tech work will follow

The vault should feel *populated*, not empty. She should be able to open it and see her business reflected back.

## Industry-specific addenda

### Pet-care / pet-sitting (tomorrow's friend)

Specific questions to surface pet-care nuance:

- **Pet types served** — dogs only? Cats? Exotic (reptiles, birds, fish)? Special needs?
- **Service types** — boarding, drop-in visits, dog walks, overnight stays, transport, training, grooming?
- **Visit cadence** — daily for some clients? Weekly? Ad-hoc?
- **Key handoff protocol** — does she hold keys, use lockboxes, smart locks?
- **Insurance + bonding** — Pet Sitters International? Local liability? Bonded for key-holding?
- **Vet partnerships** — does she have go-to vets for emergencies? Referral relationships?
- **Emergency protocols** — what happens when a pet gets injured / loose / sick during her visit?
- **Vehicle / travel** — does she travel between clients? Mileage tracked? Vehicle insured commercially?
- **Equipment owned** — leashes, treats, first aid, transport carriers, cleanup supplies?
- **Background check / certifications** — pet CPR? Pet first aid? Background check for client trust?
- **Pet info storage** — where do allergy / medication / behavior / vet info live today?
- **Photo / update protocol** — does she send daily photos? Update clients in real-time?
- **Pricing structure** — per-visit, per-day, monthly package, sliding by service?
- **Geographic radius** — how far does she travel? Strict limits?
- **Seasonal patterns** — summer/holiday peaks? Slow seasons?

## Anti-patterns

Things to avoid during the discovery session:

- **Don't lead with the methodology.** Lead with her business. The methodology is invisible scaffolding.
- **Don't translate her vocabulary to yours.** Capture in her language; harmonize later.
- **Don't fill silences too quickly.** The best answers come after a pause.
- **Don't make it feel like an interview or audit.** This is a generative conversation.
- **Don't commit to building anything mid-session.** Capture, decide between sessions.
- **Don't pre-narrow her BASED ambition** with "well, realistically..."
- **Don't pretend you have answers you don't have.** "I don't know — let's figure it out" is a strong consultant move.
- **Don't try to cover all 7 layers if it's killing the energy.** A great Layer 1-4 + Layer 7 is better than a forced full pass.
- **Don't tell her the Wonka story today.** The brand isn't ready; the methodology is doing the work.

## Variations by operator type

| Operator type | Adjustments |
|---|---|
| **Solo founder** | All layers as-is |
| **Team-based** | Add: "Walk me through your team — roles, who does what, who you trust with what." |
| **Service business** | Standard framework applies |
| **Product business** | Layer 2 emphasis on inventory / catalog / fulfillment |
| **Pre-launch** | Layer 1 → "what does a typical week look like *now*, even though the business isn't started yet?" |
| **Running business** | Standard framework |
| **Local** | Layer 2: emphasis on geographic constraints and community |
| **Online / national** | Layer 2: emphasis on channel mix and customer acquisition |

## Pre-session prep (30 minutes before)

A quick prep ritual before the session:

- [ ] Open this framework + the consultancy checklist
- [ ] Confirm her machine has Obsidian (or be ready to install)
- [ ] Confirm Claude Code / Cowork is reachable
- [ ] Have a notebook ready (in case her vault isn't accessible at start)
- [ ] Hydrate; restroom
- [ ] Set time expectation with her ("This will probably take 2-3 hours; let me know when you need breaks")
- [ ] Note your own energy — discovery work is dense; pace matters
- [ ] Make sure no other Claude session has heavy state competing for your attention

---

## After-session protocol

Within 24 hours of the discovery session:

- [ ] Synthesize the raw session notes into the artifacts listed in *Output artifacts*
- [ ] Send her the entity-profile draft + VISION_SPEC draft for review
- [ ] Schedule the follow-up session (this is when actual setup work happens)
- [ ] Add your own retrospective notes to `a consulting practice/_ideas/`: what worked, what didn't, what's missing from this framework

The framework gets better every time you use it. Refine.
