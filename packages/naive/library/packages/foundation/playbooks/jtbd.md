# Jobs of each customer profile

Draft. This playbook does not decide a venture's jobs. It walks a founder through one method and writes the result, as strings, into `jobs` on each profile record, `foundation/icp/<id>`. An agent's write of that record lands as a draft. The founder accepts it.

The method is Jobs to Be Done as Clayton Christensen and Bob Moesta describe it. A different practice, Strategyn's Outcome-Driven Innovation, uses the same family of words for a different record. This playbook does not produce that record. Trademark risk is at the end of the method section.

Each profile is its own record at `foundation/icp/<id>` (see `docs/reference/ROUTES.md`, generated by `node packages/naive/src/router/router.mjs --md`). The route `foundation/icp` lists them, keyed by id. It has read, view, and layout, and no write. Writes go to `foundation/icp/<id>`.

## What the method does

A job is the progress a person is trying to make in a particular circumstance. Christensen, Fogg, and Waldeck state it as the progress an individual seeks in a given circumstance, covering functional, social, and emotional needs that change priority with the circumstance. The circumstance is the unit of the work. ([Health for Hire](https://www.christenseninstitute.org/wp-content/uploads/2017/10/Health-for-Hire.pdf), Christensen Institute, October 2017.) Arnett, Moesta, and Horn use the same definition: the progress someone is trying to make in a particular circumstance. Progress means movement toward a goal. A job is rarely one event, and it is not only a problem, though it can be one. ([The Teacher's Quest for Progress](https://www.christenseninstitute.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/08/JTBD.pdf), Christensen Institute, September 2018.) The Institute's theory page says the same thing in public: everyone has jobs, the progress they are trying to make within particular circumstances, and a job has functional, social, and emotional dimensions. ([Jobs to Be Done Theory](https://www.christenseninstitute.org/theory/jobs-to-be-done/).)

People hire a product or a practice to help with a job that has arisen, the way they hire a contractor. They fire it when it does the job poorly. The thing they hire competes with whatever else they could hire for that progress, including a workaround and doing nothing. The Teacher's Quest paper gives the commuting smoothie as the picture: the hire competes with bananas, coffee, and a granola bar, because the circumstance is a long drive with one hand on the wheel, and the progress includes relieving stress and boredom. The customer's age band is not the unit. ([The Teacher's Quest for Progress](https://www.christenseninstitute.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/08/JTBD.pdf).)

Arnett, Moesta, and Horn credit the theory to Bob Moesta along with Clayton Christensen. It looks for what causes a person to change course and pull a new solution into their life. The useful interview is with someone who recently switched. Moesta, in a conversation with Intercom, says he wants what people did, or how they felt, and that talking to people who have not switched does not show him the cause. ([Bob Moesta on Jobs-to-be-Done](https://www.intercom.com/blog/podcasts/podcast-bob-moesta-on-jobs-to-be-done/), Intercom.)

Four forces sit on that switch. Two push toward the new hire: the push of the situation (the struggle that makes someone want to act) and the pull of the new solution (they can see how life would be better). Two hold them back: the anxiety of the new solution, and the habit of the present. ([The Teacher's Quest for Progress](https://www.christenseninstitute.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/08/JTBD.pdf); [The Forces of Progress](https://www.christenseninstitute.org/graphic/the-forces-of-progress/), Christensen Institute; the same four names in [Health for Hire](https://www.christenseninstitute.org/wp-content/uploads/2017/10/Health-for-Hire.pdf).) The Teacher's Quest paper tells Moesta's condominium interviews as the method in the wild: buyers had already bought, the clue was anxiety about leaving a life behind, and the job was the transition. The feature list was not the job. This playbook borrows that lens. It does not borrow the story, the building, or any line from it, into a venture's records.

Naive already stores this as strings. On an ICP profile, `jobs` is "What they are trying to get done (jobs to be done)" (`packages/naive/src/records/icp-profile.schema.json`). On a product card, `job` is "The need it meets" (`packages/naive/src/records/product.schema.json`). Those are different fields. This playbook fills the profile's `jobs`. It reads the product's `job` so the founder can see the claim, and the check fails if a profile job is that claim copied across.

Strategyn describes a job as "the task, goal, or objective a customer is trying to accomplish in a given situation" and applies that through Outcome-Driven Innovation, a process that stores desired outcomes as metrics. ([Jobs to Be Done](https://strategyn.com/jobs-to-be-done/), Strategyn.) Their page marks Outcome-Driven Innovation with a registration sign. This playbook does not use that name, and it does not write outcome metrics. The ICP field holds sentences of progress.

**Trademark risk.** Strategyn Holdings, LLC applied to register the mark JOBS-TO-BE-DONE (serial 86737621). The Rewired Group LLC opposed it (opposition 91226696, filed 3 March 2016). On 23 July 2016 the Trademark Trial and Appeal Board sustained the opposition. The application status is ABANDONED - AFTER INTER-PARTES DECISION. ([TTABVUE, opposition 91226696](https://ttabvue.uspto.gov/ttabvue/v?pno=91226696).) This playbook does not use the phrase as a mark. The words that land in `jobs` are the founder's, from their own customers.

## Template

Do the steps in order. Read and write through the router (`node packages/naive/src/interfaces/cli/mentura.mjs <route> <action> --venture <id>`). A missing record comes back as missing. Leave it missing. Do not fill a profile, a form, or a job from memory.

The venture provides `foundation/problem` and `foundation/icp` when the foundation package is in use (`packages/naive/library/packages/foundation/package.json` lists both as required). A product card is optional. Read `product/<id>` only after `product` shows that the card is there.

| Step | What you do | Route | Action |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Read the struggle the venture already states | `foundation/problem` | read |
| 2 | Read the profiles | `foundation/icp` | read |
| 3 | See whether a product card exists | `product` | read |
| 4 | If a card exists, read the need it claims to meet | `product/<id>` | read |
| 5 | Write `jobs` on each profile, and keep every other field | `foundation/icp/<id>` | write |
| 6 | See whether the jobs are yours yet | `purpose/foundation` | run |
| 7 | Accept each draft, after the checks pass | `foundation/icp/<id>` | write, as a person (`mentura accept`) |

### 1. Read the problem

Route: `foundation/problem`, action `read`.

Use `statement`, `segment`, `workarounds`, and `forms`. Each form has an `id` and a `statement`. `workarounds` is how people cope today (`packages/naive/src/records/validation-problem.schema.json`). That list is the current hire, including a spreadsheet or doing it by hand.

If the read is `not_found`, stop. The circumstance for a job has to be a form this record already names. Do not invent a problem.

### 2. Read the profiles

Route: `foundation/icp`, action `read`.

The result maps each profile `id` to its record. Keep each whole record, because step 5 sends it back with every field it has, not only `id`, `who`, `short`, `traits`, `problem_forms`, and `jobs`. Do not add a profile. Do not drop one. Do not change which profile is `primary`. `packages/naive/src/engine/foundation.mjs` fails when more than one profile exists and the number of primary profiles is not one.

If the read is `not_found`, stop.

`problem_forms` on a profile are ids of forms on the problem record. A later check fails when an id is not on that record. Do not add an id in this playbook.

### 3. Look for a product card

Route: `product`, action `read`. This is the list.

If the read is `not_found`, the venture has no product card. Skip step 4. A missing card is a fact. It is not a job.

### 4. Read the product's claimed job

Route: `product/<id>`, action `read`, once per id the list returned.

Note the card's `job` string when it has one. That string is the need the product claims to meet. You will use it in check 2, as a string you must not copy onto the profile. Do not write `product/<id>` in this playbook. Do not read `product/<id>` when step 3 found no card.

### 5. Write the jobs

For each profile, answer these from the records you already read and from a switch you can point to. Moesta's practice is to ask someone who already switched what they did. If you cannot point to a switch, or to a workaround already on `foundation/problem`, leave that profile's `jobs` as it was. An empty list fails check 1, which is the playbook telling you the job is not earned yet.

Write the answers as sentences in `jobs`. The schema has no field for the forces. Keep the forces in your notes and let them shape the sentence. Do not add keys.

For each job you are willing to keep:

- **Circumstance.** Name one `forms` id that this profile's `problem_forms` already lists, and use that form's statement. The sentence has to be about that circumstance.
- **Progress.** What they are trying to get done then. Movement toward a goal. Functional, and social or emotional when the switch actually showed those. The sentence is this progress, in words a customer would use.
- **Current hire.** What they use now: a line from `workarounds`, something they hired and fired, or nothing. This is context for you. It is not the job sentence, unless the progress is "keep using that workaround," which is a habit, not a job to write down.
- **Forces, in your notes.** Push of the situation. Pull of the new way, only if they can already picture it. Anxiety of the new way. Habit of the present. If anxiety or habit is the whole story, you still write the progress they are trying to make, and you do not write a feature that would "solve" the anxiety.

A profile may hold more than one job. The Teacher's Quest research found more than one job among people who look alike on a demographic line. Write one sentence per job.

Shape of a sentence, as a blank, not as copy to reuse:

`When <the form's circumstance>, <the progress they are trying to make>.`

Route: `foundation/icp/<id>`, action `write`, once per profile you are filling. Send that profile's whole record. Change only `jobs`. Leave `who`, `traits`, `problem_forms`, `primary`, and every other field as you read them. A write to `foundation/icp` is refused, because that route has no write action.

The record's decision class is `strategy-or-constitution` (`docs/reference/ROUTES.md`). An agent's write is stored as a draft under `_drafts/`, one file per profile. The live record is unchanged until the founder accepts. Say in the write note that the sentences came from this playbook and from which switch or workaround. Do not present the draft as the founder's words.

### 6. See whether the jobs are yours

Route: `purpose/foundation`, action `run`.

Read the `who` question. The statement `icp` is `draft` while any profile's draft is waiting, `agent` if an agent wrote the live record and no person has accepted it, and `confirmed` after a person accepts. `missing` means step 2 had nothing to read.

Also read `foundation/icp/<id>` for each profile you wrote. `pending_draft` means a draft file is waiting. The list route does not show it. The `result` is the live record, which does not yet contain the draft's `jobs`.

### 7. Accept

After checks 1 to 3 pass on the drafts, the founder accepts each one:

`node packages/naive/src/interfaces/cli/mentura.mjs accept foundation/icp/<profile id> --venture <venture id> --confirm foundation/icp/<profile id>`

That writes `foundation/icp/<profile id>` as a person. The typed confirmation is required because the record holds a foundation statement (`packages/naive/src/interfaces/cli/mentura.mjs`). Then run step 6 again. Check 4 passes when `icp` is `confirmed` and no profile has a `pending_draft`.

## Checks

Run these on each draft while its `pending_draft` is set (open the draft file the read names), and again on the live profiles after accept. A check that cannot fail is not a check. Each one below fails on a record you can build, and passes on a record you can build. The replay is `node packages/naive/test/verification/playbook-jtbd/replay.mjs`.

### 1. Every profile has a job

Read `foundation/icp`, and use a profile's draft in place of its live record while one is waiting. For every profile, `jobs` is an array of one or more strings, and each string has a character that is not a space.

- Pass: every profile has such a list.
- Fail: any profile has no `jobs`, an empty list, or a blank string.

`packages/naive/src/engine/foundation.mjs` does not check `jobs`. An empty list still leaves the `who` statement filled, because that statement is the profiles themselves. This check is the one that sees the empty list.

### 2. A job is not a copy of who they are, or of the product's claim

For each job string, compare the whole string.

- Fail if it equals that profile's `who`, or `short`, or any entry in `traits`.
- Fail if it equals the `job` of any `product/<id>` you read in step 4.
- Pass when none of those equalities hold.

This is an exact comparison. It catches a paste. It does not judge tone. You still read the sentence and ask whether it is progress in the circumstance from step 5. If it is a demographic or a feature list, do not accept it, even when the strings differ.

### 3. Problem forms still resolve

On a draft, compare by hand. `node packages/naive/src/engine/foundation.mjs` reads live records only, so it does not see a draft.

- Pass: every id in the draft's `problem_forms` is a form `id` on `foundation/problem`.
- Fail: an id is not on that record.

On the live profiles after accept, run `node packages/naive/src/engine/foundation.mjs <venture dir>`.

- Pass: exit 0.
- Fail: exit 1, with a line that a profile names a problem form the problem record lacks.

The template does not add form ids. This fails when a write put a new id in `problem_forms`. The live run also fails when the primary-profile count is wrong. Do not "fix" that by adding a profile.

### 4. You accepted the draft

Read `foundation/icp/<id>` for each profile and run `purpose/foundation`.

- Fail while any `pending_draft` is set, or while the `icp` statement is `draft`, `agent`, or `missing`.
- Pass when no `pending_draft` is set and the `icp` statement is `confirmed`.

Checks 1 and 2 can pass on the draft while check 4 still fails. The jobs are not the founder's until check 4 passes. Do not accept a draft that fails check 1, 2, or 3. Accepting it would make the failure the live record.

The playbook has worked when all four pass on the live record.
