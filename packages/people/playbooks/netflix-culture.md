# Netflix culture, as a method for a team plan

Draft for the founder. This playbook does not decide the culture, the bar, or a hire. You write the words. An agent may draft them. You accept them.

It borrows a method from Netflix's culture memo: publish a short document of how the team works, say who should not join, and hold every open role to a bar you can test. It does not borrow Netflix's name, its principle names, or its sentences. The records it fills are a culture doc in `people/team-plan` `note`, and a hiring bar on the open person roles of that same record.

The people function reads other functions' records by key. It does not copy their text into its own.

## What Netflix does

Netflix keeps a public culture document and revises it as the company changes. On 24 June 2024, chief talent officer Sergio Ezama wrote that Reed Hastings published a 125-page PowerPoint in August 2009, and that Netflix had revised it four times over the following 15 years. The June 2024 version took 12 months. Every employee could comment. Ezama wrote that they received over 1,500 comments, and that Netflix calls that invitation "farming for dissent." He wrote that the memo had gotten long, so they shortened it to what they consider most important and most their own. They also put back two ideas he said earlier revisions had watered down: responsibility, and the difference between good process and bad process, as against having no process. The memo is organized around four principles Netflix names for itself. Ezama describes the company the memo is for as an entertainment company of 13k+ people operating around the world. He wrote that the memo exists so someone applying for a job can see what motivates Netflix, and so employees share an account of what Netflix values. ([Sharing Our Latest Culture Memo](https://about.netflix.com/news/sharing-our-latest-culture-memo))

The current memo is the careers page [Netflix Culture](https://jobs.netflix.com/culture). It says the culture is based on four principles, which it names The Dream Team, People Over Process, Uncomfortably Exciting, and Great and Always Better. It says the document will keep changing as the business changes, and that what will not change is a focus on excellence. It tells the reader Netflix is not for everyone. The page says the first culture deck was written in 2009.

The Verge's 24 June 2024 interview with co-CEO Greg Peters says the 2009 deck was turned into a prose memo in 2017. That date is the interviewer's, in the introduction and in his question. In the transcript Peters does not repeat the year. ([The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/24182520/netflix-co-ceo-memo-ads-subscription-price-decoder-podcast-interview))

What follows is the method, in plain steps. The names in quotes are Netflix's. They are listed again under trademark risk. Do not use them as your culture's names.

**A document people read before they join.** The memo is public. It says who the company is for and who it is not for. Peters told The Verge the point of being direct is that people can decide whether they want to join. He said he would put culture above strategy and execution, because a strong culture is how a company gets better at the other two. He said the memo gets shorter as they get better at saying what matters. ([The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/24182520/netflix-co-ceo-memo-ads-subscription-price-decoder-podcast-interview))

**Behaviors, held up in public, especially for leaders.** The memo says it is easy to talk about values and harder to live them, and that Netflix works to keep people accountable, especially leaders. The values it lists are Netflix's. Your document names your own behaviors. Each one needs a test: what a person would observe if it were true.

**A performance bar for who is on the team.** The memo says Netflix models itself on a professional sports team rather than a family, because a family is unconditional and a team picks the person for the position. It says a high performer in any role is many times more effective than the average employee. That is Netflix's claim. It is not a number you should copy, and this playbook does not turn "many times" into one. The practice to borrow is: write what "strong at this role, and strong to work with" means, and say what is not enough for an offer. ([Netflix Culture](https://jobs.netflix.com/culture))

**A question the manager can answer.** The memo asks managers two questions: "if X wanted to leave, would I fight to keep them?" and "knowing everything I know today, would I hire X again?" If the answer is no, it says it is fairer to part ways quickly. It also says managers should talk about what is going well and what is not, so the answer is not a surprise, and that they judge the whole record rather than one bet that failed. A footnote says the name Netflix uses for this test came from Reed Hastings. Use your own question and your own name for it. ([Netflix Culture](https://jobs.netflix.com/culture))

**Skill does not excuse how someone treats colleagues.** The memo says there is no place for people who do not treat colleagues with decency and respect, however talented they are. ([Netflix Culture](https://jobs.netflix.com/culture))

**Pay is a judgment, recorded separately.** The memo says Netflix pays "personal top of market" for the role and location: a judgment of what that person could make in a similar role at another company, and what Netflix would pay to keep or replace them. In Naive, pay is a `spend-money` decision and equity is `ownership-or-control`. Both are a person's decision. This playbook does not set a number. If `governance/ownership` already records the terms, the brief points at that route. If it does not, the brief says pay and equity are not recorded. ([Netflix Culture](https://jobs.netflix.com/culture), decision classes in `governance/venture-template/decision-rights.json`)

**One owner for a significant decision, after disagreement.** Peters told The Verge his first question is whether a decision is his or should be made somewhere else in the company, so that people throughout the company can make decisions. The memo says managers give context and clarity so people can decide their own work. Netflix calls that "context not control." It says context is not the same as staying out of the work: managers coach, and they step in when a decision is unethical, could materially harm the company, in a crisis, or when a new person does not yet have the context. For a significant decision the memo names one person, an "informed captain," who makes the call. That person is expected to seek different opinions first. Netflix calls that "farming for dissent." It says opinions are not equal: the people closest to the work weigh more, and not everyone weighs in. After the decision, people who argued for another approach "disagree then commit." The owner then looks back at what worked. ([Netflix Culture](https://jobs.netflix.com/culture), [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/24182520/netflix-co-ceo-memo-ads-subscription-price-decoder-podcast-interview))

**Few rules, and the rules that remain are about harm.** The memo says Netflix keeps strict rules against harassment, marginalizing colleagues, leaking company information, and insider trading. Other process, it says, should be simple, efficient, and impactful. It gives its vacation policy and its expenses policy as very short sentences. Those sentences are Netflix's. Peters told The Verge that "freedom and responsibility," the name of a section in the original deck, was meant as a balance: if people are responsible, the company does not need many rules. He said people heard the freedom and missed the responsibility. He said Netflix likes a good process and rejects process whose job is to catch someone expensing something wrong. The practice to borrow: write the few rules you actually need, say what harm each one prevents, and do not add a rule whose only job is to stop a competent person from using judgment. ([Netflix Culture](https://jobs.netflix.com/culture), [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/24182520/netflix-co-ceo-memo-ads-subscription-price-decoder-podcast-interview), [Sharing Our Latest Culture Memo](https://about.netflix.com/news/sharing-our-latest-culture-memo))

**Revise the document. Do not frame it.** The memo says Netflix seeks to improve the culture rather than preserve it, and that new employees help shape how the company works. Your note names the next time you will read it again. ([Netflix Culture](https://jobs.netflix.com/culture))

The memo ends with a literary quotation. That quotation is not Netflix's to give you, and it is not yours to paste. Leave it out.

### Trademark risk

Netflix is a trademark. So are the names and sentences of this memo. Do not use them as your venture's name, your principle names, your hiring question's name, or your policy text. In particular, do not put these in `people/team-plan` or in a recruiting brief:

- Netflix
- The Dream Team, People Over Process, People over Process, Uncomfortably Exciting, Great and Always Better
- keeper test
- informed captain
- farming for dissent
- context not control
- highly aligned, loosely coupled, and loosely coupled, highly aligned
- disagree then commit
- personal top of market
- Freedom and Responsibility
- Take vacation
- Act in Netflix's best interests

Describe the practice in your own words. A note or a brief that contains one of these fails the check below.

## Template

Each step names the Naive route it reads or writes. Read a route before you use its contents. If the read comes back with no record, the venture has not provided that record. Do not fill the hole from memory, and do not paste another record's text in its place.

Keys, and where the text stays:

| Key | Route | What you may put in the team plan |
|---|---|---|
| the problem | `foundation/problem` | The route name. The statement stays on the problem record. |
| `alignment:<id>` | `foundation/alignment` | The id, as `alignment:<id>`. The statement stays on the alignment record. |
| a gap | `people/team` | The skill string, on the hiring bar of the role that covers it. |
| a person | `people`, `people/founders/[id]` | The id. The founder file stays where it is. |
| a decision class | `governance/decision-rights` | Nothing, unless that file exists and names this role as the class owner. |
| pay and equity | `governance/ownership` | The route name. Figures stay on the ownership record. |

### 1. Read the work

Routes: `foundation/problem` read, `knowledge/venture-profile` read.

Read `foundation/problem`. Read `knowledge/venture-profile`. The profile route is read only. If both reads come back with no record, stop. Write the problem with the foundation function, then come back. The culture doc is about this venture's work. It does not invent a purpose.

If the profile is missing and the problem is there, continue from the problem. Do not paste the problem statement into the team plan. You will name the route in `Rests on`.

### 2. Read alignment, and cite it by id

Route: `foundation/alignment` read.

Read `foundation/alignment`. For each principle, keep the id. You will write `alignment:<id>` in the culture doc. The statement stays in the alignment record. If the read comes back with no record, you will write `Cites: none`. Do not invent a principle to cite.

### 3. Read who is here, and the gaps

Routes: `people/team` read, `people` read, `people/founders` read, `people/founders/[id]` read.

Read `people/team`. A gap is a skill in `needed` that no `held` entry has. `schema/check-validation.mjs` lists gaps the same way. If this read comes back with no record, do not write must-have skills. Stop the hiring bar until the team record exists. You may still draft the culture doc.

Read `people`. Read `people/founders`. For each id, read `people/founders/[id]`. If the list is empty, the culture doc says who is here by saying the founders route has no files. Do not invent a person.

### 4. Read who decides, and whether pay is recorded

Routes: `governance/decision-rights` read, `governance/ownership` read.

Read `governance/decision-rights`. If the venture has no file, `schema/check-team.mjs` still uses the template at `governance/venture-template/decision-rights.json`. This playbook does not copy that template into the venture. On the role you add below, leave `authority` off. Add an authority only when the venture's own decision-rights file exists and the class's owner is this role.

Read `governance/ownership`. If the record is there, you will point the brief at the route and you will not copy a grant amount, a price, or a percent. If the read comes back with no record, the brief says pay is not recorded and equity is not recorded. Pay is `spend-money`. Equity is `ownership-or-control`. You decide those. This playbook does not.

### 5. Read the team plan before you write it

Route: `people/team-plan` read.

Read `people/team-plan`. If a record is there, keep its roles. You are adding a note and a hiring bar, not replacing the plan. If the read comes back with no record, you are creating it in the next two steps.

### 6. Write the culture doc

Route: `people/team-plan` write. Field: `note`.

Propose the decision in step 8 before this write if you want an agent's write held as a draft. See that step.

The note is the culture doc. Use these labels so the check can pass or fail. The words after the labels are yours.

```text
Behaviors:
- <a behavior, in your words>: <what a person would observe if it were true>
Not for: <who should not join>
Question: <the question you will ask before you hire someone, and again before you keep them>
Cites: alignment:<id>
Rests on: foundation/problem, people/team
Revised: <the next time you will read this, such as the next phase change>
```

`Cites` is `none` when alignment has no principles. `Cites` may list more than one id, separated by commas. One behavior line per behavior. The colon is the test. A behavior with no test fails the check.

Also say, in your own words, three practices from the method above:

- How a significant decision gets one owner, how disagreement is sought before the call, and how people who disagreed carry the decision out afterward.
- The few rules you keep, and the harm each one prevents.
- That you will revise this note rather than treat it as finished. `Revised` is that promise.

Do not paste an alignment statement, the problem statement, or any phrase in the trademark list.

### 7. Write the hiring bar

Route: `people/team-plan` write. Fields: `roles`, `recruiting`. The brief is the file at `recruiting[].brief`, a path relative to the venture folder. `schema/check-team.mjs` requires that file when an open person role is needed by the current phase or an earlier one.

Do this only for an open person role whose `needed_by` is the current phase or earlier. A later role stays `planned`. `operations/skills/team-design/SKILL.md` and `operations/skills/recruiting/SKILL.md` already say that. This playbook adds the bar. It does not send outreach. Contact and the offer stay with a person, as the recruiting skill says.

On the role, `why` is one sentence: what "strong" means for this role, including working with the people already here. Leave `authority` off unless step 4 allowed a class.

Add a `recruiting` entry: `role`, `kind` (`cofounder`, `hire`, `contractor`, `advisor`, or `investor`), and `brief`. Write the brief in the shape of `operations/templates/recruiting-brief.md`, then add this section. Must-have skills are gaps from `people/team`, not a list you make up.

```text
## Hiring bar
Role: <role id>
Must have:
- <a gap skill from people/team>
Working together: <what you would observe>
Decency: <what you would refuse even if the skill is strong>
Question: <the same question as the culture doc>
Adequate: <what is not enough for an offer>
Pay: not recorded
Equity: not recorded
```

When `governance/ownership` has a record, the last two lines are `Pay: see governance/ownership` and `Equity: see governance/ownership`. They are never a number.

`operations/skills/recruiting/SKILL.md` says an offer of equity is `ownership-or-control` and an offer of pay is `spend-money`. The brief stops before the offer.

### 8. You accept it

Route: `decisions/[id]` write, through `mentura propose` and `mentura decide`. The id is `team-plan-culture`. The router stores that item at `decisions/team-plan-culture.decision.json` (`decisions/*.decision.json`). It decides `people/team-plan.json`.

An agent proposes the decision open. A person decides it. Do not write `status: decided` yourself.

Write the proposal as JSON and run:

```bash
node interface/cli/mentura.mjs propose --venture <id> --input <file>
```

```json
{
  "id": "team-plan-culture",
  "name": "team-plan-culture",
  "question": "Do you accept the culture doc and the hiring bar on the team plan?",
  "answer": "<your words, or a draft you will approve>",
  "decides": { "record": "team-plan", "path": "people/team-plan.json" },
  "layer": "operations",
  "boundary": "fail-closed",
  "needed_in": "<the venture's current phase>",
  "status": "open"
}
```

`boundary: fail-closed` means that while this decision is open, an agent's write of `people/team-plan` is a draft. Propose this before step 6 if an agent is doing the writing. You accept the draft with:

```bash
node interface/cli/mentura.mjs accept people/team-plan --venture <id>
```

You decide it with your name. `--confirm` must be the decision id:

```bash
node interface/cli/mentura.mjs decide team-plan-culture --venture <id> --by "<your name>" --confirm team-plan-culture
```

Pass `--answer` when the words should be yours rather than the draft. After the decision is decided, a later agent write is not held by this decision. A later change is still yours: write `people/team-plan` as a person (`--as person`), or open a new decision.

## Checks

A check that cannot fail is not a check. Each one below fails on a real record and passes on a real record.

### The team-plan check

Route: `people/team-plan` check.

```bash
node interface/cli/mentura.mjs people/team-plan check --venture <id>
```

This runs `schema/check-team.mjs`. It fails when an open person role needed by the current phase or earlier has no `recruiting` entry, or the brief file is missing. It passes when that entry and that file exist.

It does not fail when there is no team plan. It does not read the culture note. A pass here is not a pass of the culture check.

### The culture check

Read `people/team-plan`, `foundation/alignment`, `people/team`, `governance/ownership`, the brief file, and `decisions/team-plan-culture`.

It fails when any of these is true:

- `note` is missing, or a behavior line has no colon, or `Not for`, `Question`, `Cites`, `Rests on`, or `Revised` is missing.
- `Cites` names an id that is not on `foundation/alignment`, or the note contains a principle's statement.
- `Cites` is not `none` when alignment has no principles.
- The note or the brief contains a phrase from the trademark list.
- An open person role needed now has a gap you meant it to cover, and that skill is not under `Must have` in its brief.
- The brief has a pay or equity figure. That includes a grant amount copied out of `governance/ownership`.
- Ownership is missing and the brief does not say pay and equity are not recorded.
- Ownership is present and the brief does not point at `governance/ownership`.
- `decisions/team-plan-culture` is missing, is not `decided`, or has no `provenance.approved_by`.

It passes when none of those are true.
