# Physical methodologies: first pass (2026-10-11)

For a private assignment (residential buildings). **Evidence standard:** search results and catalogue pages only. All catalog answers stay `assumed`.

| Entry | What was found | What was not found |
|---|---|---|
| **BIM** | ISO 19650 standardises information management across delivery (Part 2) and operation (Part 3). Part 4 (2022) says the effort of its information-exchange checks depends on asset complexity, people's experience and national policy, and that for small or simple assets many items can be set aside; it suggests templates by asset size. A 2023 study of 28 interviews on Peruvian public hospitals found budget, legal, cultural and staffing barriers | Outcome studies on small or residential projects; national guidance on proportionate use |
| **Set-based design** | Origin at Toyota: Ward, Liker, Cristiano and Sobek, "The second Toyota paradox" (1995); Sobek, Ward and Liker, "Toyota's principles of set-based concurrent engineering" (1999), from about six months of interviews. Consider broad sets of solutions, narrow gradually; slower to define early, faster to converge. A later review extends it to systems engineering | Any construction or architecture case study |
| **Space programme** | Architectural programming: Peña (first edition 1969; used by NCARB for the predesign exam from 1973), now Peña and Parshall, *Problem Seeking*, 5th ed.: goals, facts, concepts, needs, problem statement; covers BIM and integrated practice | Room-size benchmarks; empirical evidence linking room size to behaviour. Sommer's *Personal Space* (1969) is the foundational work on personal space, but nothing was retrieved this pass |

## Pass 2 (same day)

**Set-based design in construction.** Still no quantified results. Parrish's Berkeley PhD (2009) applied it to reinforcing-steel design and found practitioners use set-based thinking informally but not as a formal method. A review of set-based design research found only one action-research study in civil engineering and construction. Most directly relevant, not yet read: Castañeda et al., "Set-based design in construction projects: benefits, difficulties and trends" (IGLC 2023). **Verdict:** plausible for the venture's early phase, but not evidenced in construction. Keep its answers assumed.

**Room size, behaviour and privacy.** This changes what the space programme must contain:
- **Perceived crowding predicts harm better than density or floor area,** and privacy mediates between them (UK lockdown study, 2024). The authors argue for floor-plan quality for privacy over size.
- **Persons per bedroom** has more reliable links to health than persons per room (HUD, cited by a Beijing study). In one US child study, each additional person per usable room was associated with more internalising (+2.6%) and externalising (+4.4%) problems after adjustment.
- **Layout matters:** bedrooms close to shared rooms or bathrooms are associated with stronger feelings of crowding; small groups with a semi-private area residents can control reduce uncontrolled contact (suite-style dormitory studies). Privacy has personal, informational, territorial and acoustic dimensions.
- **Thresholds are not settled.** Figures around 15–20 m² per person recur but vary across countries and studies; do not treat any one number as a standard.
- Much of this is correlational; a 2026 systematic review (Buildings, MDPI) on residential crowding and mental health is the best next source.

**Consequence for the catalog:** the space programme's notation should record, per room, its intent, occupancy (persons per bedroom), privacy level and acoustic and visual separation, adjacency to shared rooms, and who controls the space, with area derived from these rather than set first.

**ISO 19650 full text** is paywalled; not read. Defer until a host country is chosen, then read its national annex.

## Implications for the assignment

- The pairing proposed (set-based design while site, jurisdiction and partners are unknown, then BIM for detailed design) matches what each source says each method is for. That is still an assumption for buildings.
- The space programme sits before both: it states what each room is for, which sets the constraints the alternatives are tested against.
